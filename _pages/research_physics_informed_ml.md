Materials data are costly to obtain, and models often need to predict behavior beyond the conditions used for training. Physical knowledge provides a basis for making those predictions more reliable.

Our work combines polymer physics with molecular representations to predict properties across different architectures. How monomers are connected can matter as much as composition; a model needs to capture those differences to describe material behavior.

We judge these models by whether their predictions remain accurate and physically consistent for molecules and conditions they have not seen before. This supports material design for energy dominance and the circular economy.
