Group overview

The CMPD Laboratory at the University of South Carolina uses molecular simulation and machine learning to design soft materials and chemical products.

  • Research: Energy dominance, the circular economy, and advanced manufacturing.
  • Culture: Respect each other and do rigorous science. There are no stupid questions. Just ask, help each other, and learn together.

Logistics

  • Workspace: Dr. Jiang’s office is in Swearingen 2C19. The student and postdoc office is in 3C30.
  • Computing: Request a Research Computing account.
  • Shared resources: We use Slack for everyday communication, Google Calendar for meetings and time away, and OneDrive for research documents. Contact Dr. Jiang for access.
  • Travel and vacation: Add conference travel and vacation dates to the group calendar. For planned absences longer than two weeks, notify Dr. Jiang at least two months ahead. For illness or emergencies, let him know as soon as you reasonably can. Vacations are encouraged. Work hard, have fun, and keep the group informed.

Day-to-day operations

  • Communication: Use Slack for ideas, papers, and quick questions. Send important documents, such as paper drafts and grant applications, by email so Dr. Jiang can keep a permanent record.
  • Individual meetings: We meet weekly to discuss your work and next steps. Come prepared with results, questions, and a plan. Keep your meeting slides organized by date.
  • Group meetings: We meet weekly to learn from each other, ask for advice, and practice presenting. Share a research update or literature review, and prepare as you would for an AIChE Annual Meeting presentation.
  • Progress updates: Every Monday morning, email Dr. Jiang a brief summary of your work, at least two papers you read, any problems, and your next steps. If something got in the way, explain briefly. Use this update to guide our individual meeting.
  • Working arrangements: You are expected to work in the lab and learn from your peers. For planned remote work, notify Dr. Jiang in advance and add the dates to the group calendar.

Expectations

What group members can expect from the PI

  • Mentoring: I will help you shape your project, set priorities, and work through problems. As you gain experience, I will support you in taking more ownership.
  • Availability and feedback: You do not need to wait for our weekly meeting to ask for help. I will make time for questions and give honest feedback. Send drafts and recommendation requests early, with a clear deadline, so we can agree on a timeline.
  • Development and support: We will discuss your career goals and the skills you want to build. If you are struggling with the work or workload, tell me early so we can work out a plan.

What the PI expects from group members

  • Ownership: Follow through on agreed tasks and bring ideas for next steps. Take initiative instead of waiting for instructions.
  • Learning and progress: Keep reading, build your technical skills, and take coursework seriously. Set realistic goals and document what you learn. Careful work that rules out an idea or explains a failed attempt also counts as progress.
  • Communication and teamwork: Speak up when you are stuck or disagree. Be honest about mistakes, give useful feedback, and help your labmates.

Goals and review

  • Individual plans: At the start of each semester, we will agree on a few research and learning goals. We will check progress and adjust them as the work develops.
  • Milestones: Know your degree requirements and deadlines. We will plan research, coursework, exams, and papers around those requirements and your career goals.

Research practices

  • Research records: Keep dated notes on your questions, methods, results, failed attempts, and next steps. Record enough detail for someone else to repeat the work.
  • Code and data: Keep your code on GitHub with regular commits and give Dr. Jiang access. Keep research repositories private until we agree to release them. Save inputs, software versions, and analysis steps. Use OneDrive for research documents, and agree with Dr. Jiang on storage and backups for large simulation datasets.
  • Checking results: Do not trust a result just because the code runs or the plot looks good. Check against known results, test your assumptions, and understand the uncertainty before drawing conclusions.
  • Writing and credit: We use LaTeX for papers. If you are new to it, start with the Overleaf documentation. Discuss paper plans, contributions, and authorship early. Review drafts with Dr. Jiang before sharing them outside the group.
  • AI tools: Use AI to help you understand concepts and code, especially when starting out. Do not use it to write your machine learning or simulation code. Established software and libraries, including LAMMPS, are allowed; write your own scripts and understand what they do. Create your figures and presentations yourself. AI-generated figures or visual representations are not allowed.

Getting started and moving on

Joining the group

These are the starting goals for new PhD students. We will adjust them together based on your background and project.

  • First week: Meet your labmates, get access to group and computing resources, and complete required training. Meet with Dr. Jiang to discuss your first project.
  • First month: Learn to use GitHub, access the server, run a basic machine learning algorithm, and set up and run a simple molecular dynamics simulation in LAMMPS.
  • Second month: Understand your first project’s field, the main open questions, and where your work fits.
  • End of the first semester: Have preliminary results for your first project and a plan for what comes next.

Completing a project or leaving the group

  • Handover: Organize your code, data, and notes so someone else can continue the work. Update GitHub, explain how to reproduce your results, and list unfinished tasks. Make sure Dr. Jiang has access to everything.
  • Follow-up: Agree with Dr. Jiang on plans for remaining papers and collaboration. Return group equipment and coordinate account or access changes before leaving.

Questions and updates

  • Help and concerns: Talk to Dr. Jiang if you need support or have a concern. If you would rather speak with someone outside the group, contact the graduate program director or department chair.
  • Updates: If something in this guide is unclear or no longer works, bring it up. We will revisit it together each semester.
