Why it matters

A promising molecular design still has to be made into something useful. For soft materials, flow, deformation, mixing, and temperature changes can affect molecular organization and the properties of the resulting product.

For example, a formulation may need to flow through a nozzle or spread into a coating during processing, yet maintain a different response in the finished product. Connecting those needs requires understanding both the material and the process.

This makes processing part of the materials problem. What a material is made of and what happens to it during manufacturing both matter.

What we study

We are interested in connections between molecular structure, material response, and process behavior. Broad questions include:

  • Flow and deformation: How do molecular architecture and interactions affect viscosity and relaxation?
  • Processing history: How does a material’s response depend on the conditions it experiences?
  • Process understanding: What can measurements and models tell us about product consistency and changing operating conditions?

Rheology, the study of how materials flow and deform, connects molecular behavior with many processing decisions. Process modeling adds a wider view of equipment, operating conditions, and observable outputs.

Our approach

We connect molecular and mesoscale simulation with physical analysis and machine learning. Our published polymer-design work provides examples of linking molecular architecture to flow behavior.

At the process scale, published collaborative work on digital-twin simulation and computer vision illustrates how models and measurements can support monitoring and control. A digital twin is a computational representation used to study a physical process; its predictions still need validation for the setting in which it is used.

What students learn

Students learn about rheology, simulation across scales, process modeling, and data analysis. You practice connecting an observable response with a physical explanation and asking how a change in processing might affect a product.

This theme supports energy materials and sustainability, where performance, resource use, and manufacturing constraints need to be considered together.
